Cockpit UX: audit of 3.8 and what the next pass should do

Three UX passes in August fixed the big legibility problems: the cycle is visible, the lanes are in loop order, the chat is above the fold. What is left is a different class of problem. The cockpit now shows everything; the next pass should make it show the right thing for the moment you open it, and give the arbiter tools that match how you actually use it: you leave, the agents work, you come back.

Diagnosis

Who: the arbiter, alone, on a Mac, usually with a terminal open beside the browser. Task, in your words: "how do I begin, tell the lead something, does anything need me, what is it doing, what did it cost." Since 2026-08-16 the CLI loop has been the day-to-day tool and the cockpit is parked, which is itself the strongest finding: the cockpit is not yet earning a tab.

Governing principles (four, deliberately):

  • Visibility of system status — the page should answer "what happened while I was away" without scrolling.
  • Feature consolidation (one feature, one home) — rounds, verdicts and activity currently live in two places each.
  • Von Restorff / Fitts — one primary action per screen, sized and placed for the moment; nothing destructive where the eye lands first.
  • Progressive disclosure — the Usage, Diff and Notes tabs are power surfaces; they should stay below the fold but be reachable in one keystroke.

Findings

From the 3.7.1 screenshots in docs/media/screenshots/, docs/cockpit-issues.md, and the 3.8 lanes description. Severity: blocker stops the arbiter, friction costs time every session, polish is cosmetic.

blocker
No "since you left" view. Opening the cockpit after hours shows the current state, not the delta. The arbiter's real question at 7 a.m. is "what happened, what did it cost, what needs me" and the answer is scattered across the Rounds tab, the Usage tab and the Needs-you banner. Visibility of status; recognition over recall.
a Digest card at the top when the last visit is older than N minutes: rounds since then with verdict words, phases closed, escalations, bounces, tokens and window state, one line each, dismiss with one key. Details in Features.
friction
Pause is the most prominent button on every screen, including when nothing is running. In the idle screenshot the top-right still reads Pause. It is the one filled button in the strip, so the eye lands on a control that does nothing right now. Von Restorff; error prevention.
Pause only when a turn is in flight or owed; otherwise the slot shows the state ("nothing running"). The primary action of the moment (Start / Approve / Resume / Send) is the only filled button on the page.
friction
Rounds live in two places. Each verdict is a card in the reviewer lane and a row in the Rounds tab; each lead turn is a card in the lead lane and a row under "all activity". Two homes for one thing means two places to look and two renderers to keep in sync. Feature consolidation.
The lanes are the timeline. The Rounds tab becomes "Round text" (full entries, searchable) and drops the duplicated verdict list; "all activity" becomes a filter on the lanes (show gates, show lenses), not a third list.
friction
The reviewer lane is an empty half-screen when idle. "No reviews yet…" occupies 50% of the first viewport while the lead chat, the thing you came to use, gets the other half. Empty states teach; visual hierarchy.
Idle reviewer lane collapses to a header strip that says what the reviewer will do ("gate: tests + scope · lenses: correctness, scope, verification · model: opus/medium"), giving the lead lane the width. It expands the moment a turn flips.
friction
The escalation brief is a wall of monospace. Crux, Lead position, Reviewer position, Recommendation and the two suggested rulings are in one pre block; the two commands at the bottom are the useful part and they are not clickable. Recognition over recall; Fitts.
Render the brief's sections as labeled rows (the briefer already writes them with headings). Each suggested ruling becomes a button that pre-fills the comment box with that text; Approve stays green-right, Request changes stays neutral-left.
friction
The Usage chart answers a question nobody asks. "Tokens per turn by round" with cache reads folded in shows a purple line falling and a blue line at zero. What the arbiter needs on a subscription is "how much of the window is left and how fast is it going", and per phase, "what did this cost". Match the real world.
Replace the churn line with (1) a window gauge with time-to-reset and burn rate, (2) bars per round: lead / reviewer / gate seconds and tokens side by side, (3) the phase total so far. Keep the roll-up tables under a disclosure.
friction
No keyboard path. Every action is a mouse target; there is no command palette and no shortcut for "focus the composer", "approve", "open rounds". A terminal user expects ⌘K. Jakob's law.
⌘K palette listing the same actions the confirm modal already knows (they map 1:1 to CLI commands); / focuses the composer; 1–4 switch tabs; ? shows the map.
polish
"Saloon ↗" sits in the primary strip. A theme link next to the connection status on a working surface. Hierarchy.
Move it and the version into a small "…" menu with "Open in hub", "Copy project path", "Saloon".
polish
Residual engine words on the primary path. "memory: resumed session · 2 messages", "what it said", "pre-check". Not wrong, but a new reader pauses on each. Consistent language.
Add them to the existing glossary pass: "same chat, 2 messages"; "read the review"; keep "pre-check" (it is in the glossary already).
polish
Diff tab is read-only. A QA engineer reading a scope-diff will want to say "this line" and have the note land as an interjection with a file:line anchor. Today the Notes tab is a free-text box with no context. Match the real world.
Click a line in the Diff → "leave a note here" → interjection text is pre-filled with path:line. Small, and it makes the Diff tab worth opening.

Hub

The hub is in good shape for its job (triage across projects). Two small things:

  • The Waiting · stale row prints the command to run but cannot run it, by design. Keep the design; add "copy" so it is one click, and show which machine's watcher would need starting when the project is a worktree.
  • The strip's 24h burn / 7d chips would be more useful as "window: 38% · resets 02:05" (the same gauge as the cockpit) since the window is one pool across projects; that is the number that stops you working.

Sequence for the next UX pass

  1. Digest card + last-visit tracking (the blocker). Needs a small server change (record last GET per user) and a renderer; the optional Haiku summary can come later.
  2. Primary-action rule: Pause conditional, one filled button, reviewer lane collapses when idle.
  3. Consolidate rounds/activity into the lanes; Rounds tab becomes round text.
  4. Usage tab: window gauge, per-round bars, phase total; drop the churn line.
  5. ⌘K palette and shortcuts; diff-anchored notes.
  6. Brief rendering as sections with ruling buttons.

Items 1, 2 and 4 are the ones that would bring the cockpit back into daily use; 3, 5 and 6 make it pleasant. Hallway test after 1–2: open the cockpit cold the morning after a headless run and time how long it takes to say what happened. Under ten seconds is the bar.
